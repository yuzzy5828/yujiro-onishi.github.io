Yujiro Onishi

I'm a first-year Master's student at the Intelligent Systems and Informatics Laboratory (ISI Lab) at The University of Tokyo. My core research interest is centered on one question: "How can we build intelligent autonomous robots that can think, act, and self-improve by themselves in unseen scenarios?" This requires the robots to structure and relativize observations as knowledge through interaction with the real world. In that sense, I am interested in understanding the essential constraints underlying the development of true intelligence.

I tackle this problem by integrating multiple perspectives, bridging foundational theory with physical embodiment. From a theoretical and cognitive standpoint, I investigate the fundamental structures of reasoning. Inspired by Charles S. Peirce’s concept of "abduction" and Wittgenstein’s philosophy on logical form, I explore the boundaries between logical semantics and data-driven paradigms, with a particular focus on the algebraic structures of mathematical logic as a theoretical bedrock for intelligence.

To translate these foundational insights into robotic systems, I actively work on algorithmic robot learning, specifically focusing on Reinforcement Learning and Imitation Learning. Furthermore, I strongly believe that research on embodied intelligence must be grounded in physical environments. I am dedicated to real-world robotics, continuously testing my approaches on actual hardware. Currently, I am putting this into practice as an intern at Tokyo Robotics, where I work on perceptive whole-body control for humanoid robots (engaging with this project).
