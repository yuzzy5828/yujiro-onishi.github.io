Yujiro Onishi

I am a first-year Master's student at the Intelligent Systems and Informatics Laboratory (ISI Lab) at The University of Tokyo. My core research interest is centered on a single question: "How can we build intelligent autonomous robots that can think, act, and self-improve by themselves in unseen scenarios?"
I believe that this requires robots to actively structure and relativize real-world observations into structured knowledge through interaction. In that sense, I am deeply interested in understanding the essential structural constraints required to create true intelligence.

I try to tackle this problem by integrating multiple perspectives. From a theoretical standpoint, inspired by Charles S. Peirce’s concept of abduction, Wittgenstein’s philosophy on logical form, and the remarkable reasoning capabilities of infants, I explore the boundaries between logical semantics and data-driven paradigms. Specifically, I am interested in the algebraic structures of mathematical logic as a constraint for intelligence.

In parallel, I focus on robotics research to gradually expand the physical possibilities of embodied intelligence, pushing the boundaries of what physical systems can achieve. As part of this motivation, I am currently an intern at Tokyo Robotics, where I work on perceptive whole-body control for humanoid robots (engaging in this project).

Research
Exploration-assisted Bottleneck Transition Toward Robust and Data-efficient Deformable Object Manipulation
Yujiro Onishi*, Ryo Takizawa, Yoshiyuki Ohmura, Yasuo Kuniyoshi
Arxiv Preprint, 2026
arXiv

We proposed ExBot, a novel framework that achieves robust and data-efficient deformable object manipulation under severe out-of-distribution (OOD) states. The two key concepts, standardized 'Bottleneck States' and 'Exploration Action' which serve as action for unrecognizable states, make the system successfully handles highly crumpled and self-occluded rope and cloth from variable initial configurations.
